## R5 — scenario 1 names registry positions only; a query position is not an expression child

_lead · interpretation · basis R2 · 2026-09-05T20:29Z · ratified: pending_

The delta's first scenario listed "a `with` node's body" and "a set operation's branches" among the child positions the registry knows. They are not: both are `SelectNode` positions, the registry deliberately treats a query as opaque (R2), and the RLS validator cannot even receive them (`PolicyDeclaration.using: ExprNode | null`, `ExistsNode.query: SelectNode` -- three tsc errors on the reviewer's probe, the control compiles). Ruling: the WHEN names the positions the registry actually has (a comparison's two sides, a logical operator's operands, a `not` or null-test operand, an `inList`'s operand and its values, a `between`'s operand and its two bounds, a function call's arguments, a template's interpolated expressions, a window function's own call and its partition and order keys, an aggregate filter's call and its condition, the kinds that carry no child at all, and an `exists` or `selectExpr` node standing as a leaf), and an AND bullet records that a query position -- an `exists` or `selectExpr` node's query, a `with` body, a set operation's branch -- is not an expression child: the registry stops at it, and a consumer that needs to look inside descends on its own, as the lifter does through `selectChildExprs`/`replaceSelectChildExprs`. The proof rows the piece already ran (99 rows byte-identical across the fold, depth 3 to 4) cover exactly that set, so only the sentence moves. The re-review corrected the AND bullet's path claim: `with` bodies and set-operation branches are `SelectNode | SetOpNode` positions reached by the lifter's query dispatch, and `selectChildExprs` applies at each `SelectNode` leaf, not at those positions directly.
